After sharing the "rescuing a legacy system" case, I kept reflecting on one thing: the solution I proposed was built within certain constraints.

If we step beyond those constraints, the scope of what AI can participate in and solve may be much larger than we currently understand.

## The Foundation: Self-Awareness

I've developed a deep appreciation for self-awareness recently.

At the start of this year, I began deliberately training my self-awareness. The reason was simple: I discovered that many problems aren't caused by lack of ability, but by "not being aware of what the real problem is."

So I started writing consistently to organise my thoughts, emotions, and decision logic. Through this process, I gradually found that once the foundation of self-awareness is established, many previously stuck problems start to become clear — including how to more accurately understand problems, identify true constraints, and redefine solutions.

## The Constraints We Impose on AI

Returning to the legacy system case. My conclusion was: "Delegate basic implementation to AI; reserve critical decisions for people who truly understand the technology and context."

But on reflection, this statement contained a hidden premise — we had already imposed constraints on AI:

- AI didn't know the full interaction context between me and the client
- AI didn't understand the actual usage environment
- AI couldn't operate the browser or other tools
- AI couldn't access internal company knowledge or historical decisions

Often, the problem isn't that AI can't do it — it's that AI simply wasn't given enough context.

## From Prompt to Harness

This is one of the core directions in AI Engineering over the past two years:

- **Prompt Engineering**: studying "how to ask"
- **Context Engineering**: studying "what information and memory AI should have"
- **Harness Engineering**: studying "how to make AI genuinely enter workflows and operate reliably"

Applied to the legacy rescue case: if there were a long-term AI agent that continuously received client feedback, understood the project background, was familiar with team collaboration, and could operate tools and query knowledge bases — it wouldn't just be "answering questions." It could proactively fill missing information, help clarify the real problem, and build complete context.

## The Real Challenges of Context Engineering

In practice, we still face:

- Context window limitations
- Long-term and short-term memory management
- Token cost control
- RAG accuracy
- Outdated and conflicting information
- Chunking strategies for unstructured documents

These are the genuine engineering challenges when AI moves from theory to production.
