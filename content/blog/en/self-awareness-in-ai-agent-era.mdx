Something I've been feeling more and more deeply recently:

In an era of heavy AI agent use, self-awareness may not be the only competitive advantage — but it will be a critically important one.

## What AI Has Changed

AI is rapidly changing how we work. It can help us organise information, break down problems, generate content, write code, analyse data, and even handle tasks that used to take enormous amounts of time.

Efficiency has been dramatically improved, and answers have become much easier to obtain.

But precisely because AI is so convenient, we need even more to preserve one important habit: **looking back to observe how we are thinking, judging, and learning.**

## Questions to Ask Yourself After Each Collaboration

Every time I collaborate with an AI agent, I ask myself:

- What problem did I actually solve today?
- How was this problem originally defined by me?
- How did I collaborate with AI to progressively break down this problem?
- What judgements, trade-offs, and corrections did I make along the way?
- Which answers did I choose to accept? Which did I challenge? Why?
- Where are the gaps in my understanding?
- What could I do better next time I encounter a similar problem?

## AI as a Mirror

If we only throw problems at AI and take the answers, we may become more efficient — but not necessarily more capable.

But if in every use of AI we continuously observe our own thinking paths, decision logic, and knowledge gaps, then AI becomes more than a tool. It becomes a mirror.

It helps us see:

- How we define problems
- How we pose questions
- How we judge the quality of answers
- How we correct direction
- And how we learn throughout the process

---

The future difference may not only lie in who is better at using AI, but in who can continuously deepen their understanding and capabilities through using AI.

**AI makes answers easier to get. Self-awareness makes capability actually stay.**
