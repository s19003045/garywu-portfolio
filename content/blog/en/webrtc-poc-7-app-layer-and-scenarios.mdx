The [previous article](/en/blog/webrtc-poc-6-media-plane-build-vs-adopt) finished the media-layer decision. But if you think "once LiveKit is chosen, we are basically done," you would be very wrong.

The media layer is only the foundation. **Almost all of the product value users actually feel lives in the application layer**: how you log in, how you create a meeting, who can join, how links are shared, how scheduling works. This layer — whether you self-build or adopt the media layer — **you have to build yourself**, because it is your product.

## regardless of the media choice, you build this layer yourself

Lay out the features we wanted from the start, and they all land in the application layer, almost unrelated to the media layer:

- **Identity and accounts**: Google login (OIDC) → create your own user and session.
- **Meetings as first-class entities**: store meetings in a database (e.g. Postgres) — id, code, host, passcode, expiry, settings. "Share a link" = share a meeting code; "create a link" = open a meeting record.
- **Pre-create / scheduling**: add a scheduled time, recurrence, and calendar integration to the same meeting table; a link can be created ahead and only become joinable at its time.
- **Permissions and roles**: host / co-host / participant / viewer; a lobby, lock room, kick, force-mute.

And all of this grows naturally on top of what you already have in Phase 6: **the join token evolves from "room-level" to "identity + room + role + capability" level.** Phase 6's SFU already validates the `room` in the token; it extends naturally to validating `role` and permissions. The media layer (LiveKit or self-built) only decides "admit or not, may publish or not" — **what decides the permissions is your application layer.**

## the architecture beyond the media layer

```
 Google ──OIDC──▶ ┌───────────────┐  meetings/users/perms ┌──────────┐
 user   ◀─session─│  Auth/API svc │◀──────────────────────│ Postgres │
        │ w/ token │ create meeting│                       └──────────┘
        ▼         │ schedule, sign │  token + node address
   media layer (LiveKit or self-built SFU cluster) ── recording/egress → object storage / CDN
```

The new protagonists are the **Auth/API service** (login, manage meetings and permissions, sign media tokens) and the **database** (the source of truth for meetings and permissions; Redis is only suited to expirable real-time state, not the meeting entity itself). Note this layer is **completely decoupled from your media-layer choice** — which is exactly why it is the most worthwhile thing to invest in yourself.

## how scenarios reshape this layer

Even within the "application layer," the three scenarios want very different things:

**Open large-scale (Meet-like)**
- Identity: Google / social login, low friction.
- Links: anyone can create a room, share by code, guests can join.
- Focus: consumer-grade UX, **abuse prevention** (rate limiting, anti-spam, anti-harassment), reporting mechanisms.

**Security-strict enterprise**
- Identity: **enterprise SSO (their own IdP, usually not Google)**, tied to the company directory.
- Security: **E2EE, data residency / on-prem, full audit logs, device control**; recording may be outright banned or strictly governed.
- Links: not public, internal only, possibly requiring re-authentication to enter.

**10k concert**
- Identity: **ticket validation** (only ticket holders can watch), not traditional accounts.
- Scale: hundreds of thousands of viewers; the focus is **CDN cost** and concurrency.
- Features: DVR / replay, and chat/interaction that can withstand a massive audience (usually on a separate messaging system).

## one table to wrap up the three scenarios

| Dimension | Open large-scale (Meet) | Security-strict enterprise | 10k concert |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shape of scale | Many small/medium rooms (A) | Small/medium, highly sensitive | Single huge room (B) |
| Media layer | Adopt LiveKit | LiveKit on-prem + TURN | Broadcast LL-HLS/CDN |
| Identity | Google login | Enterprise SSO | Ticket validation |
| Compliance | Standard | E2EE / on-prem / audit | Licensing / ticketing |
| Biggest cost | Node scale | Compliance and ops | CDN traffic |
| Link semantics | Public, easy to share | Internal, controlled | Ticket = right to enter |

The same words "video system" actually hide three different products.

## what this whole series wants to say

Looking back across Phase 1 to Phase 6, and then these three decision articles, my biggest takeaway is not "I learned SFU" or "I learned LiveKit," but a higher-level judgment:

**From POC to product, what matters most is not mastering one technology to the extreme, but first clarifying the goal and context, then letting the goal pick the technology.**

Self-building up to Phase 6 gave me the ability to see what each option solves; laying out the three scenarios showed me how the same knowledge derives three different architectures. Technology is the means, the goal is the direction — that is probably the one sentence this whole series, from "turning on the camera" all the way to "how to choose," most wants to leave behind.

## glossary

- **OIDC**: OpenID Connect, an identity layer on top of OAuth2; Google login is one example.
- **SSO**: single sign-on, where enterprises use their own identity provider (IdP) for unified login.
- **E2EE**: end-to-end encryption, where even the server cannot see the media content.
- **IdP**: Identity Provider (e.g. a corporate directory, or Google).
- **DVR**: the ability to rewind/time-shift during a livestream.
