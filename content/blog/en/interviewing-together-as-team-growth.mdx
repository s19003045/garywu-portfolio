I've always enjoyed inviting colleagues to join interviews together.

Many people think of interviews as just one step in the hiring pipeline. But I've gradually realised: **interviews are actually an excellent environment for developing team capability.**

## The Extra Value of Multi-Person Interviews

In many companies, interviews are concentrated among managers or a small number of senior members. Beyond the obvious benefits — multi-angle candidate assessment, evaluating "team fit" — there's additional value when team members participate:

- Practising how to clearly articulate their own thoughts
- Practising how to observe and understand a candidate
- Practising turning familiar technical knowledge into spoken language
- Practising thinking through questions from a resume
- Observing how different people ask questions, interact, and make judgements

These are capabilities that don't always get deliberately trained in daily work — but they surface naturally in the interview setting.

## The Debrief After the Interview

I particularly enjoy the post-interview discussion with colleagues:

- What did we observe just now?
- Which answers are worth thinking about?
- Which questions actually reveal something about our own understanding?

Sometimes, the learning from an interview doesn't only happen in the candidate. It happens in the interviewers themselves. Especially when you start needing to "guide," "question," and "explain" — you rediscover that many things you consider familiar can't actually be explained clearly.

## Interviews as More Than Filtering

Interviews simultaneously serve as:

- **Team culture showcase**: candidates experience the team atmosphere from the interview itself
- **Knowledge exchange opportunity**: candidates familiar with different domains bring new application contexts
- **Capability development space**: for junior engineers, observing how senior members ask questions is valuable learning
- **Self-reflection process**: re-examining your own technical understanding from an external perspective

---

I later realised that sometimes, the sense of achievement in seeing others grow isn't less than completing something yourself.

Interviews are a place where you can simultaneously see: where others have reached — and where you are yourself.
